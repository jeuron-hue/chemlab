    Bond polarity

    The more electronegative atom attracts the bonding electrons more strongly and gains a partial negative charge, δ−. The larger the electronegativity difference, ΔEN, the more polar the bond.

    Pauling electronegativities. 9476 does not require quantitative treatment of electronegativity: the numbers are for illustration.

    non-polar covalentpolar covalentionic
    • The change from non-polar covalent to polar covalent to ionic is gradual. There is no sharp cut-off value of ΔEN.
    • ΔEN is only a guide: HF (ΔEN = 1.78) is a polar covalent molecule, and AlCl3 (ΔEN = 1.55) has considerable covalent character. See the Fajans panel below.

    EnrichmentCovalent character in ionic compounds (Fajans' rules)

    Beyond the 9476 syllabus. A small, highly charged cation (high charge density) distorts, or polarises, the electron cloud of a large anion. Electron density is drawn into the region between the ions, giving the bond some covalent character.
