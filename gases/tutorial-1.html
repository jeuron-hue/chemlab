Use R = 8.31 J K−1 mol−1, L = 6.02 × 1023 mol−1, 1 atm = 101 325 Pa, and molar volumes of 22.7 dm3 mol−1 at s.t.p. and 24 dm3 mol−1 at r.t.p. (Data Booklet). Relative atomic masses are from the Data Booklet.

Question 1

  1. State the basic assumptions of the kinetic theory as applied to an ideal gas.
  2. Use the kinetic theory to explain how a gas exerts a pressure on the walls of its container.
  3. A fixed mass of gas is sealed in a rigid container and heated. Explain, in terms of the particles, why its pressure increases.
  4. Two flasks at the same temperature contain helium and argon. Compare the average kinetic energies and the average speeds of the two kinds of atom.

Question 2

The gas volume in a cylinder is 6.2 cm3 and its pressure is 141.86 kPa. The piston is then pushed in until the gas volume is 3.1 cm3 while the temperature remains constant. Calculate the pressure after the change in volume.

Question 3

A balloon will burst at a volume of 2.00 dm3. The balloon is partially filled at a temperature of 20 °C and a pressure of 103.35 kPa to a volume of 0.75 dm3. Assuming that the pressure remains constant, at what temperature will the balloon burst?

Question 4

A 2.07 dm3 cylinder contains 2.88 mol of helium gas at 22 °C. What is the pressure of the gas in the cylinder?

Question 5

A potato has been stuck in a car’s 4.5 dm3 exhaust pipe, which is initially at s.t.p. and has the same concentration of gas particles as the air. When the teacher starts the car, the temperature quickly increases to 80 °C and remains at that temperature as the exhaust pipe fills with more and more gas molecules. The potato will shoot out of the exhaust pipe when the pressure reaches 2.53 MPa. How many gas molecules must be in the exhaust pipe at this point?

Question 6

A weather balloon has a volume of 52.5 dm3 at a temperature of 295 K. The balloon is released and rises to an altitude where the temperature is 252 K.

  1. How does this temperature change affect the motion of the gas particles?
  2. The original pressure at 295 K was 1.00 atm and the pressure at the higher altitude at 252 K is 0.45 atm. What is the new volume of the balloon?

Question 7

0.120 g of a volatile liquid, Y, was injected into a gas syringe kept at 100 °C. The vapour occupied 30.8 cm3 at a pressure of 101 kPa. Y contains 10.0 % carbon, 0.8 % hydrogen and 89.1 % chlorine by mass.

  1. Calculate the relative molecular mass of Y.
  2. Find the empirical formula of Y, and hence its molecular formula.
  3. Explain why the syringe must be kept well above the boiling point of Y (61 °C).

Question 8

Sketch graphs to show how, for a fixed mass of an ideal gas,

(i) the pressure, p, varies with the volume, V, at constant temperature;

(ii) the product pV varies with p, at constant temperature;

(iii) the volume varies with the temperature in °C, at constant pressure. Mark the value at which the extrapolated line meets the temperature axis.

Question 9

A yellow-green gas has a density of 2.86 g dm−3 at 25 °C and 100 kPa. Calculate its relative molecular mass and identify the gas.

Question 10

0.240 g of magnesium ribbon reacts with excess dilute hydrochloric acid:

Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl2(aq) + H2(g)

  1. Calculate the volume of hydrogen produced, in cm3, at r.t.p.
  2. Calculate the volume of hydrogen produced, in cm3, at 30 °C and 99.0 kPa.
