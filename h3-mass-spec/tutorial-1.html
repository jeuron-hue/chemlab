H3.4.T1 §1

Question 1

This question is about the mass spectrum of methanol, CH3OH.

  1. Write an equation, with state symbols, for the formation of the molecular ion of methanol in a mass spectrometer. [1]
  2. State the m/z of the molecular ion, and explain why it is equal to the Mr of methanol. [2]
  3. The base peak of the spectrum is at m/z 31. Identify the ion responsible, and write an equation for its formation from the molecular ion. [2]
  4. Explain why the other product of the reaction in (c) gives no peak in the spectrum. [2]

H3.4.T1 §2

Question 2

A compound Y is either a hydrocarbon or a ketone. Its mass spectrum has a molecular ion peak at m/z 86 with a height of 15.0, and a peak at m/z 87 with a height of 1.00.

  1. Explain why there is a peak at m/z 87. [2]
  2. Calculate the number of carbon atoms in a molecule of Y. Show your working. [2]
  3. Hence decide whether Y is C6H14 or C5H10O (both have Mr 86). Explain your answer. [2]

Three compounds X, Y and Z all give a molecular ion peak at m/z 72. They are pentane, C5H12; butanone, C4H8O; and propenoic acid, C3H4O2, in some order. The heights of their M and M+1 peaks are:

  1. Calculate the number of carbon atoms in each compound. [3]
  2. Hence identify X, Y and Z. [3]

H3.4.T1 §4

Question 4

The table gives the main peaks in the simulated mass spectra of four halogen compounds. The ratios are the relative heights of the peaks in each cluster.

CompoundPeaks (m/z, with ratio)
bromomethane, CH3Br94, 96 (1 : 1); 15
1,2-dichloroethane, ClCH2CH2Cl98, 100, 102 (9 : 6 : 1); 63, 65 (3 : 1); 49, 51 (3 : 1)
dibromomethane, CH2Br2172, 174, 176 (1 : 2 : 1); 93, 95 (1 : 1)
chlorobenzene, C6H5Cl112, 114 (3 : 1); 77
  1. For the molecular ion of each compound, state how the pattern of peaks shows the number and type of halogen atoms present. [4]
  2. Identify the ions at m/z 63 and 65 from 1,2-dichloroethane and at m/z 93 and 95 from dibromomethane, and the radical lost in each case. [2]
  3. Explain why the peak at m/z 77 from chlorobenzene is a single peak, not a pair. [2]

H3.4.T1 §5

Question 5 (Further example)

Bromodichloromethane, CHBrCl2, is one of the by-products formed when drinking water is treated with chlorine.

  1. Its molecular ion gives four peaks, at m/z 162, 164, 166 and 168. Show that the heights of these peaks are close to the ratio 9 : 15 : 7 : 1. [4]
  2. Predict the m/z values, and the approximate ratio of heights, of the clusters given by the fragment ions CHCl2+ and CHBrCl+. [2]

H3.4.T1 §6

Question 6

The simulated mass spectrum of 2-methylpropane, (CH3)3CH, shows a small peak at m/z 58, the base peak at m/z 43 and a small peak at m/z 15.

  1. Identify the ions at m/z 58, 43 and 15. [2]
  2. Write an equation for the formation of the ion at m/z 43 from the molecular ion. [2]
  3. Explain why the peak at m/z 43 is so much taller than the peak at m/z 15. [2]

Question 7

Fig 1 shows the simulated mass spectra of the two isomers of C5H12 named pentane, CH3CH2CH2CH2CH3, and 2,2-dimethylpropane, C(CH3)4.

  1. Decide which spectrum belongs to which isomer. Give your reasons, identifying the ions at m/z 57, 43 and 29. [4]
  2. Write an equation for the formation of the ion at m/z 57 from 2,2-dimethylpropane, and explain why this ion is so stable. [2]
  3. Suggest why there is no molecular ion peak in one of the spectra. [2]

H3.4.T1 §9

Question 9 (Further example)

Read the passage and answer the questions that follow.

Caffeine, C8H10N4O2, gives a molecular ion at m/z 194. Its concentration in a drink can be measured by LC-MS with an internal standard: caffeine in which three 12C atoms have been replaced by 13C ("13C3-caffeine", m/z 197). 5.00 µg of the standard is added to 1.00 cm3 of the drink. In the LC-MS run, the peak area at m/z 194 is 4.20 × 105 and the peak area at m/z 197 is 5.00 × 105 (illustrative data). Assume that equal masses of caffeine and of the standard give equal peak areas.

  1. Explain why the labelled caffeine behaves in the same way as caffeine in the instrument, yet can be measured separately. [2]
  2. Explain why a standard three units heavier is used, rather than one containing a single 13C atom. [2]
  3. Calculate the mass of caffeine in 1.00 cm3 of the drink, and its concentration in mg dm−3. [3]

H3.4.T1 §10

Question 10 (Further example)

Read the passage and answer the questions that follow.

A GC-MS run on a degreasing solvent shows an unexpected peak. Its mass spectrum (Fig 3) has a weak cluster at m/z 132, 134, 136 and 138, with relative heights 100 : 96 : 31 : 3, and stronger clusters at m/z 117, 119, 121 (with a tiny 123) and at m/z 97, 99, 101. A library search suggests 1,1,1-trichloroethane, CH3CCl3.

  1. Show that a molecular ion containing three Cl atoms gives four peaks in a ratio close to 27 : 27 : 9 : 1, and that this agrees with the data. [3]
  2. Identify the ions at m/z 117 and 97, and the radical lost in forming each. [2]
  3. Draw the structures of 1,1,1-trichloroethane and its isomer 1,1,2-trichloroethane. Explain why the isomer would give a cluster at m/z 83, 85 and 87 but no peak at 117. [2]
  4. Explain why the analyst still injects an authentic sample of 1,1,1-trichloroethane, and state how the 1H NMR spectra of the two isomers would differ. [2]
