Use Kw = 1.00 × 10−14 mol2 dm−6 at 298 K (Data Booklet). Other constants are given in each question (literature values). Give pH to 2 decimal places and other answers to the significant figures of the data, with units.

10.T2 §1

Question 1

25.0 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 aqueous ammonia (Kb = 1.8 × 10−5 mol dm−3) is titrated with 0.100 mol dm−3 hydrochloric acid, which is added from a burette.

  1. Calculate the pH (i) at the start, (ii) after 12.5 cm3 of acid has been added, (iii) at the equivalence point, (iv) after 50.0 cm3 of acid has been added.
  2. Sketch the titration curve, marking these four points.
  3. Suggest a suitable indicator from the table in Part 2, and explain your choice.

10.T2 §2

Question 2

  1. Explain, with an equation, why the solution at the equivalence point of a titration of ethanoic acid with sodium hydroxide is alkaline.
  2. 20.0 cm3 of 0.150 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid (Ka = 1.8 × 10−5 mol dm−3) is titrated with 0.150 mol dm−3 sodium hydroxide. Calculate the pH at the equivalence point.
  3. Calculate the pH of 0.100 mol dm−3 ammonium chloride (Kb(NH3) = 1.8 × 10−5 mol dm−3).
  4. Explain why a solution of sodium chloride has pH 7.00 at 298 K.

10.T2 §4

Question 4

Bromocresol green is a weak acid, HIn, that is yellow; its conjugate base In− is blue. It changes colour between pH 3.8 and 5.4 (literature).

  1. Write the equilibrium for the indicator in water, and use it to explain why the indicator is yellow in acid and blue in alkali.
  2. Estimate pKIn for bromocresol green.
  3. Using the steep sections in Part 2, state whether bromocresol green is suitable for each of the four titrations of Fig 1, giving a reason.
  4. Explain the difference between the end point and the equivalence point of a titration.

10.T2 §5

Question 5

  1. What is meant by a buffer solution?
  2. A buffer contains ethanoic acid and sodium ethanoate. Explain, with equations, how it keeps the pH almost constant when a small amount of (i) hydrochloric acid, (ii) sodium hydroxide is added.
  3. Explain why a solution of ethanoic acid alone is not a good buffer.
  4. Explain why a mixture of hydrochloric acid and sodium chloride is not a buffer.

10.T2 §6

Question 6

A buffer contains 0.150 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid and 0.250 mol dm−3 sodium ethanoate. Ka = 1.8 × 10−5 mol dm−3.

  1. Calculate the pH of the buffer.
  2. 2.00 cm3 of 1.00 mol dm−3 hydrochloric acid is added to 100 cm3 of the buffer. Calculate the new pH.
  3. Calculate the pH if 2.00 cm3 of 1.00 mol dm−3 sodium hydroxide is added to another 100 cm3 of the buffer instead.

10.T2 §7

Question 7

10.0 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 hydrochloric acid is added to 40.0 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 aqueous ammonia (Kb = 1.8 × 10−5 mol dm−3).

  1. Explain why the resulting solution is a buffer.
  2. Calculate its pH.
  3. What volume of the hydrochloric acid would have to be added to 40.0 cm3 of the ammonia to give a buffer of pH 9.26? Explain.

10.T2 §8

Question 8

A buffer of pH 4.00 is needed. Methanoic acid has Ka = 1.8 × 10−4 mol dm−3.

  1. Explain why methanoic acid and sodium methanoate are a better choice for this buffer than ethanoic acid and sodium ethanoate (pKa 4.74).
  2. Calculate the ratio [HCOO−] / [HCOOH] needed.
  3. Calculate the mass of sodium methanoate, HCOONa (Mr 68.0), that must be dissolved in 500 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 methanoic acid. Ignore any change in volume.

10.T2 §9

Question 9

  1. Write equations to show how carbon dioxide from the air produces hydrogencarbonate and carbonate ions in sea water.
  2. Explain, with equations, how the HCO3− / CO32− system keeps the pH of the oceans almost constant.
  3. Explain how the rapid increase in atmospheric carbon dioxide has led to ocean acidification, and why this harms organisms that build shells of calcium carbonate.
  4. The pH of the surface ocean has fallen from about 8.2 to about 8.1. Calculate the factor by which [H+] has increased.

10.T2 §10

Question 10 (Further example)

Blood plasma is buffered mainly by H2CO3 and HCO3−. Under the conditions in the body, pKa of H2CO3 may be taken as 6.1 (literature).

  1. Calculate the ratio [HCO3−] / [H2CO3] in blood at pH 7.40.
  2. Use your answer to suggest whether this buffer is better at removing added acid or added alkali.
  3. During strenuous exercise, lactic acid enters the blood. Write an equation for the reaction that removes the extra H+, and suggest how breathing faster helps.
