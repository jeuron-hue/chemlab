Hammond explorer

Drag ΔH for one step and watch the transition state slide towards whichever stable species is nearer to it in energy: early and reactant-like for an exothermic step, late and product-like for an endothermic one. The curves come from a simple model with illustrative numbers, so read the trend, not the values.

Presets

The Hammond postulate. A transition state resembles the stable species (reactant, intermediate or product) that is nearest to it in energy.

In an exothermic step the transition state comes early and resembles the reactants; in an endothermic step it comes late and resembles the products (or the intermediate formed).

EnrichmentThe model treats reactant and product as two parabolas that cross at the transition state (λ = 240 kJ mol−1, so Ea = 60 kJ mol−1 when ΔH = 0). Model numbers: real reactions have different intrinsic barriers.

Explorer · 9813 3.3(a)(i)

E2 in 3D: anti-periplanar

Turn the back carbon about the C–C bond until a β-hydrogen is anti-periplanar to Br (H–C–C–Br dihedral 180°), then eliminate and see which alkene forms. The read-outs and the (E)/(Z) and (R)/(S) labels are computed from the model's coordinates; try the four stereoisomers of 2-bromo-3-methylpentane to see a stereospecific elimination.

Substrate

Base: the ethoxide ion, C2H5O− (NaOC2H5 in ethanol, heat). It removes the β-hydrogen as H+ while Br− leaves, in one step.
