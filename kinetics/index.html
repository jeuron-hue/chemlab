Boltzmann distribution

Kinetic energies of gas molecules, f(E) ∝ √E · e−E/RT. The area under every curve is the same: the same number of molecules.

Always below Ea.
Reference frozen at T1 = 400 K. Move the temperature slider to compare.
Energy axis range / kJ mol−1
Boltzmann distribution
High-energy tail, magnified
  • Higher T flattens the curve and moves the peak to the right; the area is unchanged. The fraction with E ≥ Ea rises sharply, so k increases. Collision frequency also rises, but that is the minor effect.
  • A catalyst does not change the curve. It provides an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy, so a larger fraction of molecules has E ≥ Ea(cat), giving a larger k.
  • The curve starts at the origin, is not symmetrical, and approaches the energy axis without ever touching it.

    Worked example

    A first-order reaction has t½ = 20 min. What fraction remains after 1 hour?

    1. n = 60 min ÷ 20 min = 3 half-lives
    2. Fraction remaining = (½)3 = 1/8
    3. i.e. 12.5 % remains

    Catalysis examples

    A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway (mechanism) with lower activation energy, and is regenerated at the end. Homogeneous: catalyst in the same phase as the reactants. Heterogeneous: catalyst in a different phase.

    Haber process

    Heterogeneous different phase: solid Fe, gaseous reactants
    N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g)
    1. Adsorption: N2 and H2 adsorb onto active sites on the Fe surface.
    2. Weakening of bonds: bonds within the adsorbed molecules, including N≡N, are weakened.
    3. Reaction on the surface: adsorbed N and H atoms react to form NH3.
    4. Desorption: NH3 leaves the surface, freeing the active sites.

    Catalytic converter

    Heterogeneous different phase: solid Pt/Pd/Rh on a ceramic honeycomb, gaseous reactants
    2CO(g) + 2NO(g) → 2CO2(g) + N2(g)

    It also oxidises CO and unburnt hydrocarbons, for example:

    2CO(g) + O2(g) → 2CO2(g)
    C8H18(g) + 12½O2(g) → 8CO2(g) + 9H2O(g)

    Same surface sequence as the Haber process: adsorption, weakening of bonds, reaction, desorption. The honeycomb gives a large surface area.

    Atmospheric NO2 in SO2 oxidation

    Homogeneous same phase: catalyst and reactants all gaseous
    SO2(g) + NO2(g) → SO3(g) + NO(g)
    NO(g) + ½O2(g) → NO2(g)
    overall: SO2(g) + ½O2(g) → SO3(g)

    NO2 is used in step 1 and regenerated in step 2, so it does not appear in the overall equation.

    Fe2+ in the S2O82− / I− reaction

    Homogeneous same phase: catalyst and reactants all aqueous
    1. S2O82−(aq) + 2Fe2+(aq) → 2SO42−(aq) + 2Fe3+(aq)
    2. 2Fe3+(aq) + 2I−(aq) → 2Fe2+(aq) + I2(aq)
    3. overall: S2O82−(aq) + 2I−(aq) → 2SO42−(aq) + I2(aq)

    The uncatalysed reaction is slow because the two anions repel. Each catalysed step is between oppositely charged ions. Fe3+ also works because the cycle simply starts at the second step.
