Ar: H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, I 126.9 (Data Booklet). Molar gas volume 24 dm3 mol−1 at r.t.p. (Data Booklet). Boiling points are literature values.

11.6.T1 §1

Question 1

There are four alcohols with molecular formula C4H10O.

  1. Draw the structural formula of each, and name it.
  2. Classify each as primary, secondary or tertiary.
  3. Which of the four has a chiral centre?
  4. Give the structural formula of one compound with formula C4H10O that is not an alcohol.

11.6.T1 §2

Question 2

Write balanced equations, with state symbols, for

  1. the complete combustion of ethanol;
  2. the complete combustion of propan-2-ol;
  3. the incomplete combustion of ethanol to carbon monoxide and water.

11.6.T1 §3

Question 3

  1. Write an equation for the conversion of ethanol into bromoethane, and state the reagent and condition.
  2. Propan-2-ol reacts with phosphorus(V) chloride at room temperature. Write the equation, name the organic product and describe what you would see.
  3. Explain why the apparatus and the propan-2-ol must be dry.
  4. Explain why the reaction with PCl5 cannot be used to show that an unknown compound is an alcohol rather than a carboxylic acid.

11.6.T1 §4

Question 4

  1. Write an equation for the reaction of ethanol with sodium. Give two ways in which what you see differs from the reaction of sodium with water.
  2. Calculate the volume of hydrogen, at r.t.p., given off when 1.15 g of ethanol reacts with excess sodium.
  3. When water is added to the solution left after the reaction, universal indicator turns purple. Explain, with an equation.

11.6.T1 §5

Question 5

Propan-1-ol (b.p. 97 °C) is oxidised by acidified potassium dichromate(VI). Propanal boils at 48 °C and propanoic acid at 141 °C.

  1. Name the organic product when the mixture is heated with distillation, and explain why distillation gives this product.
  2. Name the organic product when the mixture is heated under reflux with excess oxidising agent.
  3. Write equations, using [O], for the reactions in (a) and (b).
  4. State the colour change of the oxidising agent. What would you see if acidified potassium manganate(VII) were used instead?

11.6.T1 §6

Question 6

Give the structural formula of the organic product, if any, when each alcohol is heated under reflux with acidified K2Cr2O7. Explain any case where there is no reaction.

  1. butan-2-ol
  2. 2-methylpropan-2-ol
  3. cyclohexanol, C6H11OH

11.6.T1 §7

Question 7

6.00 g of propan-1-ol was heated under reflux with excess acidified potassium manganate(VII). 5.55 g of propanoic acid was obtained. Calculate the percentage yield.

11.6.T1 §8

Question 8

  1. State the reagent and conditions for the dehydration of an alcohol to an alkene.
  2. 2-Methylbutan-2-ol, CH3CH2C(OH)(CH3)2, is dehydrated. Draw and name the two alkenes formed.
  3. Explain why neither of the alkenes in (b) shows cis-trans isomerism.
  4. Explain why propan-2-ol gives only one alkene.

11.6.T1 §9

Question 9

For each pair, describe one simple chemical test that would distinguish the two compounds. Give the reagent and conditions, and what you would see with each compound.

  1. propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol
  2. propan-2-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol
  3. ethanol and methanol

11.6.T1 §10

Question 10

  1. Which of these alcohols give a pale yellow precipitate with warm alkaline aqueous iodine? Explain your choice in each case: methanol, ethanol, propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol, pentan-3-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol, 1-phenylethanol (C6H5CH(OH)CH3).
  2. Write the overall equation for the reaction of propan-2-ol, and name the organic ion formed.
  3. Calculate the greatest mass of tri-iodomethane that 0.600 g of propan-2-ol could give.

11.6.T1 §11

Question 11

Compound P, C5H12O, fizzes with sodium. P is oxidised by warm acidified potassium dichromate(VI) to Q, which gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNPH but does not give a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent. P gives a pale yellow precipitate with warm alkaline aqueous iodine. When P is dehydrated, three alkenes form, two of which are a cis-trans pair.

  1. Explain what each observation tells you about P.
  2. Identify P and Q, and draw the three alkenes.
  3. 3-Methylbutan-2-ol, (CH3)2CHCH(OH)CH3, also fits the first three observations. Explain why it is not P.

11.6.T1 §12

Question 12 (Further example)

3-Methylbutane-1,3-diol has the structure HOCH2CH2C(CH3)2OH.

  1. Classify each of its –OH groups as primary, secondary or tertiary.
  2. Give the structural formula of the organic product when it is heated under reflux with excess acidified K2Cr2O7.
  3. How many moles of hydrogen are given off when 1 mol of the diol reacts with excess sodium?
  4. Would the diol give a precipitate with warm alkaline aqueous iodine? Explain.
