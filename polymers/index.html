        Why · 11.10(e), (f)

        No polar link against a polar link

        The C=O carbon of an ester or amide link is δ+, so water, H+, OH− or an enzyme can attack it. A C–C/C–H backbone has no such site.

        Bond energies · Data Booklet

        Bonds in the chains

        Strength is not the whole story: C–O (360) and C–N (305) are not much different from C–C (350), but they are polar, so the link they sit in can be hydrolysed.
