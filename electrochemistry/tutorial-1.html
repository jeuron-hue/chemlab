12.T1 §1 · 16/10/26

Question 1

Find the oxidation number of the element shown in each species.

  1. S in H2SO4, SO2 and Na2S2O3
  2. N in N2O, HNO2 and N2H4
  3. Cl in Cl2O7 and ClO2
  4. Cr in CrO42−; O in H2O2; H in CaH2
  5. C in C2O42−
  6. V in VO2+ and in VO2+

12.T1 §2 · 16/10/26

Question 2

State whether each reaction is a redox reaction. For each redox reaction, identify the oxidising agent and the reducing agent, giving the changes in oxidation number.

  1. Zn(s) + 2Ag+(aq) → Zn2+(aq) + 2Ag(s)
  2. Cr2O72−(aq) + 2OH−(aq) ⇌ 2CrO42−(aq) + H2O(l)
  3. 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g)
  4. Ag+(aq) + Cl−(aq) → AgCl(s)
  5. SO2(g) + 2H2S(g) → 3S(s) + 2H2O(l)
  6. Cl2(aq) + H2O(l) ⇌ HCl(aq) + HOCl(aq) (Further example): what type of redox reaction is this?

12.T1 §3 · 16/10/26

Question 3

Write balanced half-equations for these changes in acidic solution, and check the number of electrons against the change in oxidation number.

  1. SO42− → SO2
  2. NO3− → NH4+
  3. VO3− → VO2+
  4. H2O2 → O2
  5. CH3CHO → CH3COOH
  6. (Further example) Cr3+ → CrO42− in alkaline solution.

12.T1 §4 · 16/10/26

Question 4

Use half-equations to construct balanced ionic equations for these reactions in acidic solution.

  1. Manganate(VII) ions oxidise sulfur dioxide to sulfate ions, and are reduced to Mn2+.
  2. Dichromate(VI) ions oxidise tin(II) ions to tin(IV) ions.
  3. Hydrogen peroxide oxidises iodide ions to iodine.
  4. (Further example) Peroxodisulfate ions, S2O82−, oxidise Mn2+ ions to MnO4− ions, and are reduced to sulfate ions.

12.T1 §5 · 16/10/26

Question 5

Propan-2-ol, CH3CH(OH)CH3, is oxidised to propanone, CH3COCH3, by acidified potassium dichromate(VI) (Topic 11.6).

  1. Write the half-equation for the oxidation of propan-2-ol.
  2. Construct the balanced ionic equation for the reaction.
  3. State the colour change, and the change in oxidation number of the carbon atom that is oxidised.

12.T1 §6 · 16/10/26

Question 6

25.0 cm3 of a solution of iron(II) sulfate, acidified with dilute sulfuric acid, needs 22.40 cm3 of 0.0200 mol dm−3 potassium dichromate(VI) to reach the end point.

  1. Construct the ionic equation for the reaction from half-equations.
  2. Calculate the concentration of Fe2+ in the solution.

12.T1 §7 · 16/10/26

Question 7

20.0 cm3 of a 0.100 mol dm−3 solution of the ion X3+ is oxidised by exactly 16.0 cm3 of 0.0500 mol dm−3 acidified potassium manganate(VII). The manganate(VII) is reduced to Mn2+.

  1. Calculate the amounts of X3+ and of MnO4− that react.
  2. Calculate the number of electrons gained by the manganate(VII), and hence the number lost by each X3+ ion.
  3. Deduce the new oxidation state of X.

12.T1 §8 · 16/10/26

Question 8

The concentration of chlorate(I) in bleach is found by iodometry. 10.0 cm3 of bleach is diluted to 250 cm3 with water. A 25.0 cm3 portion is added to excess potassium iodide and acidified. The iodine formed needs 18.60 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 sodium thiosulfate.

  1. Write the half-equation for the reduction of ClO− to Cl− in acid, and use it to construct the equation for the reaction of ClO− with I− in acid.
  2. Write the equation for the reaction of iodine with thiosulfate ions.
  3. Calculate the concentration of ClO− in the original bleach, in mol dm−3, and the concentration of NaClO in g dm−3.

12.T1 §9 · 16/10/26

Question 9 (Further example)

When a solution of sodium chlorate(I) is heated, this reaction takes place: 3ClO−(aq) → 2Cl−(aq) + ClO3−(aq).

  1. Give the oxidation number of chlorine in each species.
  2. Name this type of reaction, and explain your answer.
  3. Show that the electrons lost and gained balance.

12.T1 §10 · 16/10/26

Question 10

  1. Find the oxidation number of carbon in CH4, CH3OH, HCHO, HCOOH and CO2.
  2. Write half-equations, in acidic solution, for the three steps by which methanol is oxidised to methanoic acid and then to carbon dioxide.
  3. Ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid. Find the change in oxidation number of the carbon atom bonded to oxygen, and write the half-equation.
