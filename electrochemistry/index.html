Redox equation builder

Each half-equation is balanced by the four steps of the notes (Part 1): the element changing oxidation number, O with H2O, H with H+, then charge with e−.       Explorer · 9476 12(g), 12(j), 12(k)

      E and concentration

      Apply Le Chatelier's Principle to the half-equation written as a reduction: more of the oxidised form (or of H+ where it is on the left) pushes the equilibrium right, and E becomes more positive; less of it makes E more negative. The numbers come from the Nernst equation, which is beyond the syllabus: open the Enrichment panel to see them.

      Bench · 9476 12(l)–(o)

      Electrolysis bench

      Electrolysis uses electricity to drive a reaction that would not go by itself. The anode is positive (oxidation); the cathode is negative (reduction). In water, water competes: at the cathode the species with the more positive E⦵ is reduced; at the anode the species with the less positive E⦵ is oxidised. Inert electrodes are graphite or platinum.
