Diatomic MO builder

Build the MO diagrams of H2, He2, O2, F2 and two dioxygen ions, and read off the bond order, magnetism, HOMO and LUMO. Each MO is a definite energy level of the molecule, filled exactly like atomic orbitals: aufbau (lowest first), Pauli (at most two electrons, opposite spins) and Hund (degenerate MOs singly first, parallel spins). For the second-period molecules only the valence 2s and 2p MOs are drawn: the 1s core MOs (σ1s2 σ*1s2) lie far lower, are both full and cancel.

3D MO viewer

Watch two atomic orbitals combine in phase and out of phase into σ and π MOs, in three dimensions. MOs are formed by combining (adding and subtracting) atomic orbitals: a linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO). Two AOs give two MOs. In phase (the lobes that meet have the same sign) gives a bonding MO; out of phase gives an antibonding MO, written with a star. The internuclear axis is z.

π ladder

The π MOs of ethene, buta-1,3-diene, hexa-1,3,5-triene and benzene: their nodes, energies, HOMO and LUMO. The p orbitals on adjacent sp2 carbons combine into one π system: N p orbitals give N π MOs. Each MO is drawn as a p orbital on every carbon, lobe size in proportion to its share of the MO; the nodes counted are the extra nodal planes perpendicular to the molecular plane. Linear polyenes are drawn as a straight chain for clarity. Tap an MO to see it large.

Photon and spectrum converter

Convert between wavelength, frequency, wavenumber and photon energy, and place the photon on the electromagnetic spectrum. Light travels as waves of wavelength λ and frequency f, linked by c = fλ, and is absorbed or emitted in packets called photons. The energy of one photon is E = hf = hc/λ; for a mole of photons, multiply by L. Wavenumber ν̃ = 1/λ, in cm−1. Constants from the Data Booklet: h = 6.63 × 10−34 J s, c = 3.00 × 108 m s−1, L = 6.02 × 1023 mol−1.

      Energy levels

      Match a photon to the kind of energy level it can change, and see absorption and emission lines. Energy levels are discrete. A photon is absorbed only if its energy hf exactly matches the gap ΔE between two levels: ΔE = hf. The molecule is promoted to the higher level; when it drops back, a photon of the same energy is emitted. The photon from the converter above is matched to the kind of level it fits.
