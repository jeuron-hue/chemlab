    Isotopes · 6(a), 6(c)

    Relative atomic mass from isotopes

    Ar = Σ(relative isotopic mass × abundance) / Σ(abundance). Isotopic abundances are literature values, not from the Data Booklet.

      Balancer and limiting reagent · 6(f), 6(g)(i), 6(h)

      Balance it, then react it

      Change the coefficients (the big numbers in front), never the formulae. The equation is balanced when every element has the same number of atoms on each side, and, for an ionic equation, the total charge is the same on each side too.

        Formula finder · 6(d)–(e)

        Empirical and molecular formulae

        The empirical formula is the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element; the molecular formula is the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule. Find moles of each element, divide by the smallest, then multiply up if the ratio is not yet whole.

            Gas-volume burner · 6(g)(ii), 6(h)

            Burning a hydrocarbon in excess oxygen

            A hydrocarbon CxHy is sparked with excess oxygen, cooled back to r.t.p., then shaken with NaOH(aq). By Avogadro's law, equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure hold equal numbers of molecules, so volume ratios are mole ratios: CxHy + (x + y/4)O2 → xCO2 + (y/2)H2O.

              Virtual titration · 6(g)(iii), 6(h)

              Titration bench

              Do a rough titration first, then accurate ones, going drop by drop near the end point. Record each result; when two or more accurate titres agree to within 0.10 cm3 (concordant), use their mean in the calculation.

              Results

              Burette readings to the nearest 0.05 cm3, to 2 decimal places. Titre = final reading − initial reading.
