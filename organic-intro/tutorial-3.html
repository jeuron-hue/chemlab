Question 1

Classify each reaction as addition, substitution, elimination, condensation, hydrolysis, oxidation or reduction. Where you can, give the full name (for example, electrophilic addition).

  1. CH3CH=CH2 + HBr → CH3CHBrCH3
  2. C6H6 + HNO3 → C6H5NO2 + H2O (with concentrated H2SO4)
  3. CH3CH2CH2Br + OH− → CH3CH2CH2OH + Br−
  4. CH3CH2Br + OH− → CH2=CH2 + H2O + Br− (in hot ethanol)
  5. CH3COCl + CH3OH → CH3COOCH3 + HCl
  6. CH3CH2CHO + [O] → CH3CH2COOH
  7. CH3COCH3 + 2[H] → CH3CH(OH)CH3
  8. CH4 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + HCl (in UV light)
  9. CH3CONH2 + H2O + HCl → CH3COOH + NH4Cl

Question 2

  1. Classify each compound as primary, secondary or tertiary: (i) 2-chloro-2-methylbutane; (ii) 1-chloropentane; (iii) 3-bromopentane; (iv) 2-methylpropan-1-ol; (v) cyclohexanol.
  2. 2,2,4-Trimethylpentane, (CH3)3CCH2CH(CH3)2, is used as the standard for the octane rating of petrol. How many of its carbon atoms are primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary?

Question 3

  1. Classify each species as a nucleophile or an electrophile: H2O, NH3, Br+, CN−, NO2+, CH3CH2+, OH−, H+.
  2. Explain why NH3 is a nucleophile even though it is not charged, and why it can also be described as a Lewis base.
  3. Bromomethane reacts with OH− ions. Which atom in bromomethane is attacked, and why?

Question 4

Methane reacts with chlorine in ultraviolet light.

  1. Write equations for the initiation, two propagation and three termination steps that form chloromethane.
  2. Explain why the product is a mixture, and suggest how the proportion of chloromethane could be increased.
  3. A trace of ethane is found among the products. Explain how it forms and why it supports the mechanism.
  4. Explain why methane does not react with chlorine in the dark at room temperature, and why it does not react with aqueous sodium hydroxide or acidified potassium manganate(VII).

Question 5

Propene reacts with bromine dissolved in tetrachloromethane.

  1. Describe, with a curly-arrow mechanism, how the reaction takes place. Name the product.
  2. State what you would see.
  3. Explain why alkenes react readily with electrophiles but alkanes do not.

Question 6

Cyclohexene decolourises bromine at room temperature in the dark. Benzene does not, but it reacts with bromine when iron(III) bromide is added.

  1. Write equations for the reactions of cyclohexene and of benzene with bromine.
  2. Explain, in terms of delocalisation, why benzene is less reactive than cyclohexene towards bromine.
  3. Write an equation to show how FeBr3 generates the electrophile, and describe the two steps by which the electrophile reacts with benzene.
  4. Explain why benzene undergoes substitution rather than addition.

Question 7

  1. Describe, with a curly-arrow mechanism, the reaction of 1-bromobutane with aqueous hydroxide ions. Name the mechanism and write the rate equation.
  2. 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane reacts with aqueous hydroxide ions by a different mechanism. Name it, write the two steps and the rate equation.
  3. Use the ideas of carbocation stability and steric hindrance to explain why 1-bromobutane does not react by the mechanism in (b), and 2-bromo-2-methylbutane does not react by the mechanism in (a).

Question 8

1-Chlorobutane, 1-bromobutane and 1-iodobutane were warmed in separate test tubes with aqueous silver nitrate and ethanol.

  1. State the order in which precipitates appear, and the colour of each precipitate.
  2. Write an ionic equation for the formation of one of the precipitates.
  3. Explain the order using bond energies from the Data Booklet.
  4. Predict what you would see with 1-fluorobutane, and explain.

Question 9

1-Chlorobutane is hydrolysed when it is boiled with aqueous sodium hydroxide, but chlorobenzene is not. Explain this difference.

Question 10

Propanone reacts with hydrogen cyanide in the presence of a trace of sodium cyanide.

  1. Describe, with a curly-arrow mechanism, how the reaction takes place. Name the type of mechanism and the product.
  2. Explain why a trace of sodium cyanide is needed.
  3. Ethanal reacts in the same way. Its product has a chiral centre, yet the product mixture does not rotate plane-polarised light. Explain why.
