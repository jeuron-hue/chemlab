Use bond energies from the Data Booklet. Other values are given in the questions (literature values). Ar: H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0. Molar gas volume 24 dm3 mol−1 at r.t.p.

11.4.T1 §1 · 14/10/26

Question 1

  1. Write equations, with state symbols, for (i) the complete combustion of ethane, (ii) the incomplete combustion of ethane to carbon monoxide.
  2. The enthalpy change of combustion of butane, C4H10, is −2877 kJ mol−1, and that of methane is −890 kJ mol−1 (literature values). For each fuel, calculate (i) the energy released per gram, (ii) the mass of CO2 formed for every 1000 kJ of energy released.
  3. Explain, in terms of the composition of the two fuels, why methane gives less CO2 per kJ.

11.4.T1 §2 · 14/10/26

Question 2

Ethane reacts with bromine in ultraviolet light.

  1. Write equations for the initiation step, the two propagation steps and three termination steps. Name the mechanism.
  2. State two things you would see.
  3. Explain why a mixture of products forms, and suggest how the proportion of bromoethane could be increased.
  4. A trace of butane is found among the products. Explain how it forms and why its presence supports the mechanism.

11.4.T1 §3 · 14/10/26

Question 3 (Further example)

  1. Use Data Booklet bond energies to calculate ΔH for each propagation step in the chlorination of ethane and in the bromination of ethane.
  2. Use your answers to explain why ethane reacts much more slowly with bromine than with chlorine, even though the Br–Br bond is weaker than the Cl–Cl bond.

11.4.T1 §4 · 14/10/26

Question 4

Each alkane below is chlorinated in UV light. Ignoring any further substitution, how many different monochloro products (constitutional isomers) can form from each? Name them, and say which, if any, have a chiral centre.

  1. propane
  2. butane
  3. 2-methylpropane
  4. 2,2-dimethylpropane
  5. (Further example) Propane has six primary and two secondary hydrogen atoms. If every H atom were equally likely to be replaced, what would be the ratio of 1-chloropropane to 2-chloropropane? Suggest why the real ratio is different.

11.4.T1 §6 · 14/10/26

Question 6

  1. Describe, with a curly-arrow mechanism, the reaction between ethene and hydrogen chloride. Name the mechanism and the product.
  2. When ethene is bubbled into bromine water, the main product is 2-bromoethanol, not 1,2-dibromoethane. Explain why.
  3. Ethene is bubbled into bromine water containing dissolved sodium chloride. Name three organic products, and explain why 1,2-dichloroethane is not one of them.

11.4.T1 §7 · 14/10/26

Question 7

  1. State Markovnikov's rule.
  2. Give the structural formula and name of the major product, and of the minor product, when hydrogen bromide adds to (i) propene, (ii) but-1-ene, (iii) 2-methylpropene, (iv) 2-methylbut-2-ene. Explain why (v) but-2-ene gives only one product.
  3. Use the stability of the carbocation intermediates to explain the major product in (b)(iii).
  4. The 2-bromobutane formed in (b)(ii) has a chiral centre but does not rotate plane-polarised light. Explain why.

11.4.T1 §8 · 14/10/26

Question 8

Propene reacts with steam in the presence of a phosphoric(V) acid catalyst.

  1. Name the major and the minor product.
  2. Describe the mechanism, showing the role of the acid catalyst.
  3. The reaction is reversible and fewer moles of gas are formed. State and explain the effect of increasing the pressure on the yield of the alcohol.
  4. Use Data Booklet bond energies to estimate ΔH for C2H4(g) + H2O(g) → C2H5OH(g).

11.4.T1 §9 · 14/10/26

Question 9

  1. State the reagents and conditions for the hydrogenation of ethene, and the type of catalysis involved.
  2. 0.0100 mol of a hydrocarbon, C10H16, reacts completely with 480 cm3 of hydrogen, measured at r.t.p. How many C=C bonds does each molecule contain? Deduce how many rings it has, and give the molecular formula of the product.
  3. Suggest why vegetable oils are partially hydrogenated to make margarine.

11.4.T1 §10 · 14/10/26

Question 10

But-2-ene, CH3CH=CHCH3, is treated separately with the two reagents below. For each, give the organic product, an equation using [O], and what you would see.

  1. cold, alkaline KMnO4
  2. hot, acidified KMnO4
  3. Describe a simple test to tell hexane from hex-1-ene, and the result for each.

11.4.T1 §11 · 14/10/26

Question 11

Each alkene below was heated with acidified potassium manganate(VII). Deduce the structural formula and name of each alkene from its products.

  1. C5H10: carbon dioxide and butanone
  2. C5H10: propanoic acid and ethanoic acid. Can you tell whether the alkene was the cis or the trans isomer? Explain.
  3. C5H10: propanone and ethanoic acid
  4. (Further example) C6H10: a single product, hexanedioic acid, HOOC(CH2)4COOH
  5. (Further example) C6H10: carbon dioxide (2 mol per mole of alkene) and butanedioic acid, HOOCCH2CH2COOH

11.4.T1 §12 · 14/10/26

Question 12

  1. Explain how each of these forms in a petrol engine, and give one harmful effect of each: (i) carbon monoxide, (ii) nitrogen monoxide, (iii) unburnt hydrocarbons.
  2. Write equations for two reactions that remove these pollutants in a catalytic converter.
  3. Explain why a catalytic converter does nothing to reduce the enhanced greenhouse effect.
  4. Name two greenhouse gases other than carbon dioxide, and give one human source of each.
  5. Explain what is meant by the enhanced greenhouse effect.
