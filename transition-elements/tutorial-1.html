Use the Data Booklet for atomic radii, ionisation energies and Ar values. Melting points and densities are literature values, given where needed. Keep unrounded values until the last step.

13.T1 §1

Question 1

  1. Define the term transition element.
  2. Use electronic configurations to explain why scandium is a transition element but zinc is not, although both are in the d block.
  3. The copper atom has a full 3d subshell. Explain why copper is still a transition element.

13.T1 §2

Question 2

Write the electronic configurations, using [Ar], of

  1. Co;
  2. Co2+;
  3. V2+;
  4. Sc;
  5. V3+;
  6. Cr2+;
  7. Mn3+;
  8. Ni.
  9. Identify two pairs of species in (a) to (h) that are isoelectronic (have the same number of electrons).

13.T1 §3

Question 3

Use the Data Booklet values of atomic radius and first ionisation energy for chromium to copper and for lithium to fluorine.

  1. Compare the change in atomic radius, and the change in first ionisation energy, across Cr to Cu with the changes across Li to F. Quote the ranges.
  2. Explain the difference.
  3. The first ionisation energy of iron (762 kJ mol−1) is higher than that of calcium (590 kJ mol−1), although the electron removed is a 4s electron in both. Explain why.

13.T1 §4

Question 4 (parts (b) and (c) Further example)

Nickel melts at 1455 °C and has a density of 8.91 g cm−3; calcium melts at 842 °C and has a density of 1.55 g cm−3 (literature values).

  1. Explain why nickel has the higher melting point.
  2. (Further example) Assuming the atoms of both metals pack in the same way, use Ar values and atomic radii from the Data Booklet to estimate the ratio density(Ni) / density(Ca). Compare your answer with the ratio of the literature densities.
  3. (Further example) Suggest one reason why the estimate does not agree exactly.

  2. [Ar]3d34s2

13.T1 §7

Question 7

Find the oxidation number of the transition element in

  1. TiO2 and TiCl3;
  2. V2O5;
  3. CrO3;
  4. K2MnO4;
  5. (Further example) K2FeO4, potassium ferrate.

13.T1 §8

Question 8

In aqueous solution, iron(II) ions are readily oxidised by air to iron(III), but cobalt(II) ions are not, and simple cobalt(III) salts are rare. Explain this using the electronic configurations of Fe2+ and Co2+ and the third ionisation energies of iron (2960 kJ mol−1) and cobalt (3230 kJ mol−1).

13.T1 §9

Question 9

  1. Write the electronic configurations of Zn and Zn2+.
  2. Zinc forms compounds only in the +2 oxidation state. Use the first three ionisation energies of zinc from the Data Booklet to explain why.
  3. Predict whether zinc compounds are coloured. (Part 2 explains why.)

13.T1 §10

Question 10

State whether each statement is true or false, and give a reason.

  1. Transition elements lose their 3d electrons before their 4s electrons when they form ions.
  2. The highest oxidation state of every first-row transition element equals its number of 4s and 3d electrons.
  3. Transition metals are denser than calcium mainly because their atoms are heavier.
  4. Chromium has the configuration [Ar]3d44s2.
  5. The highest oxidation state of titanium is +4.
