Use h = 6.63 × 10−34 J s, c = 3.00 × 108 m s−1 and L = 6.02 × 1023 mol−1 (Data Booklet), and the colour wheel of Part 2 (Fig 13). The Qualitative Analysis Notes are in the Data Booklet.

13.T2 §1

Question 1

  1. Define the terms ligand and complex.
  2. Which of these can act as ligands: H2O, NH3, Cl−, CN−, CH4, NH4+, CO? Explain your choices.

13.T2 §2

Question 2

For each complex, give the oxidation number of the metal, the coordination number, the shape and the bond angle between neighbouring ligands.

  1. [Co(NH3)6]3+
  2. [Ni(H2O)6]2+
  3. [CoCl4]2−
  4. [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]+
  5. [Co(NH3)5Cl]2+
  6. [Fe(CN)6]4−

13.T2 §3

Question 3

Green [Ni(H2O)6]2+(aq) reacts with excess aqueous ammonia to give blue-violet [Ni(NH3)6]2+(aq).

  1. Write an equation for the reaction, and name the type of reaction.
  2. Explain why the colour changes. (You do not need to know which ligand gives the larger splitting.)

13.T2 §4

Question 4

In concentrated hydrochloric acid, pink [Co(H2O)6]2+ ions form blue [CoCl4]2− ions. The forward reaction is endothermic.

[Co(H2O)6]2+(aq) + 4Cl−(aq) ⇌ [CoCl4]2−(aq) + 6H2O(l)

  1. State the change in coordination number and in shape.
  2. Explain why the coordination number changes.
  3. State and explain what you would see if the blue solution were (i) diluted with water, (ii) warmed.

13.T2 §5

Question 5

Use the Qualitative Analysis Notes to identify the cation in each solution. Each solution contains one of Cr3+, Cu2+, Fe2+, Fe3+ or Mn2+.

  1. Aqueous sodium hydroxide gives an off-white precipitate, which quickly darkens on standing in air. The precipitate does not dissolve in excess.
  2. Aqueous sodium hydroxide gives a grey-green precipitate, which dissolves in excess to give a dark green solution.

Write ionic equations for the reactions in (a) and (b), and explain in one sentence why the precipitate in (a) darkens in air.

13.T2 §6

Question 6

  1. Describe how oxygen is carried by the iron in haemoglobin.
  2. Explain why carbon monoxide is poisonous.
  3. Explain, using an equilibrium, why a victim of carbon monoxide poisoning is given pure oxygen to breathe.

13.T2 §7

Question 7

Ni2+ forms the octahedral complex [Ni(H2O)6]2+. Draw an energy diagram to show how the 3d orbitals of Ni2+ split in this complex, and put in the electrons. Explain why two of the orbitals are raised in energy relative to the other three.

13.T2 §8

Question 8

Explain why

  1. [Sc(H2O)6]3+ is colourless;
  2. copper(I) iodide, CuI, is white, although copper(II) compounds are coloured;
  3. zinc salts are white.

13.T2 §9

Question 9

  1. A complex absorbs light most strongly at 600 nm (illustrative data). Calculate the energy gap ΔE, in J per ion and in kJ mol−1. State the colour of the light absorbed and the colour of the complex.
  2. Another complex has ΔE = 180 kJ mol−1. Calculate the wavelength of light it absorbs, and predict its colour.

13.T2 §10

Question 10 (Further example)

Adding aqueous thiocyanate ions, SCN−, to aqueous iron(III) ions gives a blood-red complex, [Fe(SCN)(H2O)5]2+ (written [Fe(SCN)]2+ in Topic 9).

  1. Write an equation for the formation of the complex from [Fe(H2O)6]3+, and explain why it is a ligand exchange.
  2. Predict and explain what you would see if more aqueous iron(III) ions were added to the equilibrium mixture.
