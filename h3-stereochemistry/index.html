Newman rotor

Turn the back carbon of ethane or butane and watch the energy change as the molecule passes through staggered and eclipsed conformations. Energies are relative to the most stable conformation; the values at the staggered and eclipsed points are literature values, and the curve between them is a schematic model drawn through those points, as in the notes.

Explorer · 9813 2.1(b)(i)

Cyclohexane in 3D

Flip a cyclohexane ring through the half-chair, twist-boat and boat and see every axial bond become equatorial, while up stays up. Pick a substituted ring to see which groups are axial, count the 1,3-diaxial interactions in each chair and find the more stable chair. The ring is an idealised model (C–C 0.154 nm, C–H 0.109 nm, C–C–C about 111°, literature); energies are literature values, and the flip path between the stations is schematic.

    Explorer · 9813 2.1(c)

    Complex isomers

    Reflect a complex in a mirror and try to superimpose the mirror image on the original by turning it. The lab searches every rotation for the best fit and reports how far apart the atoms still are: zero means superimposable (achiral), anything larger means a pair of enantiomers. The models use idealised shapes and model bond lengths, with the en rings flat.

    Complex (notes)

    Reflect first: the mirror image is the reflection of the complex (every x coordinate reversed); no turning can make a reflection. Then drag either model to turn it.
